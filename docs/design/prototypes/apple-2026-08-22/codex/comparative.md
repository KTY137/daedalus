## Ranking

1. **Sequoia — build this.**  
   It is the only prototype that looks like functioning desktop software rather than a design concept. The project navigation, Ikarus, Codebase and Knowledge form a coherent macOS split view. Operational state, provenance, lenses, command palette, receipts, council opinions and status line are all immediately findable. It is dense, but the hierarchy survives.

2. **Lagerfeld.**  
   The strongest visual point of view. Its graph treatment is more expressive than Sequoia’s, and the typography feels deliberately art-directed. But the enormous “Daedalus” masthead wastes the most valuable cockpit space, the chat is cramped, and much of the graph is too faint to read. It is a great poster for the product, not yet the best product.

3. **Keynote.**  
   Clean, calm and superficially Apple-like. Unfortunately, it looks like a presentation slide or marketing page. The mission title—not the Codebase—is the hero, the graph is tiny, and Knowledge has effectively disappeared. Too much valuable space is decorative emptiness while critical product structure is compressed.

4. **visionOS.**  
   Do not build this. It is exactly the dark floating-glass AI cockpit the brief warns against. Low contrast, clipped content, excessive material effects and disconnected floating navigation make it impressive for ten seconds and tiring thereafter. It looks generated because it substitutes atmosphere for hierarchy.

## Direction

Build **Sequoia** as the foundation.

Borrow:

- From **Lagerfeld**: progressive graph emphasis—dim unrelated nodes and make the selected subgraph unmistakable. Also borrow some of its typographic confidence.
- From **Keynote**: more breathing room around the mission and clearer stage progression.
- From **visionOS**: restrained translucency for genuinely floating surfaces only—the command palette and settings sheet. Nothing else.

The single biggest risk is **density**. Sequoia already feels close to an aircraft dashboard: five vertical regions, tiny labels and too many simultaneously visible facts. If more features are added without progressive disclosure, the graph will stop being the hero and the app will become a database administration screen.

**Least AI-looking: Sequoia.** It accepts the boring disciplines of real software—persistent navigation, conventional split views, consistent alignment, predictable controls and clear information ownership. The other three visibly try to impress. Sequoia looks like somebody actually had to use it every day.
