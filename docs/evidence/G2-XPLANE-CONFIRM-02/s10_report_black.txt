Absence of a difference is not evidence of equivalence: an INCONCLUSIVE
verdict means the run was too small or too noisy to decide, and must not
be read as a pass. Underpowered and budget-asymmetric comparisons are
withheld rather than reported as results.

UNDECIDABLE is not INCONCLUSIVE. INCONCLUSIVE says this data could have
decided and did not, so a larger run would help. UNDECIDABLE says this
data could never decide it: the object the criterion names, or the
observation that separates the two arms, is not in the input. Enlarging
such a run shrinks the interval around a comparison that cannot move,
which walks towards a verdict without measuring anything.
